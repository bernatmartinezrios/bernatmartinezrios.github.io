The problem

Deposits are lost for lack of evidence, not for lack of reason. In Spain, 20.2% of households rent their home (INE). When the contract ends, the landlord can only keep part of the deposit by proving damage, and the tenant only gets it all back by proving the state of the flat at move-in. The landlord has one month to return it (Spain's Urban Leases Act).

Hardly anyone keeps that evidence in time: it's scattered across WhatsApp, email and the phone's gallery. Existing management software is built for professionals and treats the tenant as a guest.

The competitor isn't other apps, it's WhatsApp. Logging something in Tu llavero has to be as quick as sending a photo.

The solution

A shared, neutral record: both sides see the same thing and nobody can delete what the other adds. Each feature answers a moment in the tenancy.

Moving in

A guided move-in report, room by room, with meter readings. The other side confirms it or adds an objection.

During the tenancy

Repairs with a date and "seen" status, written agreements, confirmed rent payments, reminders, documents and contacts.

Moving out

A move-out report compared with the move-in one, a countdown to the legal one-month deadline for the deposit, and a claim letter if it's missed.

Always

Jargon-free guides and export of the full record as a PDF.

My role

The whole project, on my own and in my spare time. The idea comes from my own experience renting, and the entire project, from design to code, was built with AI support: Claude Design for the brand and screens, and Claude Code to build the app.

  • Strategy: research into the market and deposit rules, competitors, MVP scope and business model.
  • UX/UI: information architecture, tenant and landlord flows, 43 high-fidelity screens and an interactive prototype.
  • Brand and design system: name, symbol, logo, palette, type, tokens and components with dark mode.
  • Content: all the app's copy, guides and posts for Instagram, LinkedIn and TikTok.
  • Development: directing the build of the real app with Claude Code, feature by feature, and testing every flow.
  • Accessibility and privacy: a WCAG 2.2 AA review and a design built around the GDPR.

Process

  1. Research. A product plan. Key decision: moving out and the deposit are there from day one, because that's where the tenant has money at stake.
  2. Design. 43 screens and a design system, with a prototype to test the flows before writing code.
  3. Build. The real app, with a database, access rules and a chained history.
  4. Review. Full walkthroughs with both roles, 320 px screens, dark mode and automated accessibility testing.
  5. Launch. Case study, social media and a waiting list.

Design decisions

  • Neutral by design. No colour, word or order favours either side. Conflict words are avoided: it says "difference", not "fault".
  • The record is the product. Every entry shows who, when and whether the other side has seen it. Nothing is deleted: withdrawing something leaves a trace.
  • As fast as WhatsApp. Reporting a repair or confirming something is one tap from the home screen.
  • The tenant doesn't depend on the landlord. They can create the record alone and invite the landlord later.
  • No passwords. You sign in with a 6-digit code by email, because magic links don't open the installed app on iPhone.
  • Colour with meaning. Green for the main action, terracotta only for warnings and deadlines, and colour is never the only signal.

Accessibility and trust

  • WCAG 2.2 AA on every screen, with both roles, in light and dark, at 320 and 360 px. No failures in automated testing (axe), with keyboard and zoom checked by hand.
  • Every event carries a SHA-256 fingerprint chained to the previous one: if anyone changed a comma, the chain would no longer match.
  • Data hosted in the EU with no third-party cookies. No ID, bank account or payslips are requested.

Status and next steps

MVP complete, tested end to end with sample data and installed on a test iPhone. Before opening it up, it still needs EU photo storage, email notifications, a legal review and testing with the first users from the waiting list.

What I learned

  • Designing all 43 screens before coding made the build fast and true to the design.
  • In a product that serves as evidence, trust is designed: date, author and "seen" must always be visible.
  • Speaking to both sides without taking a side means weighing every word.
  • With AI tools, one person with no budget can take a product from idea to a working app.
