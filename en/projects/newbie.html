The problem

More than half of Spain's population plays sports every week (CSD, Survey of Sports Habits), but doing it with others on a regular basis still depends on WhatsApp groups. It's hard to find people at your level and to coordinate spots and times, and someone who is new to a sport or to a city has no natural way in.

The problem isn't the facilities or the players. It's the social coordination layer.

Who it's for

  • Beginners who worry about not being good enough. They are the focus of the design: level is a first-class filter.
  • People who have just moved to a city.
  • Anyone who wants to turn sport into a social habit.

The opportunity

  • More than 6 million people play padel in Spain, and around 74% of bookings are open matches between players who didn't know each other.
  • Lack of time and organisation is the main barrier to playing sports (26.3%, CSD).
  • 25.7Mpeople who play sports in Spain (TAM)
  • 6Murban players of social sports (SAM)
  • 150Kactive users as a 3-year goal (SOM)

Working estimates based on public data, not results.

My role

The idea started as a project for my Master's in UX/UI Design, which I did with my classmate Alba. Building on that concept, I adapted and improved the design with Claude Code, and then led the move to a real app: I set the scope of each phase, reviewed every one on an iPhone and approved any changes to the original design.

The solution

A simple loop: discover, join, meet and repeat. Finding a nearby activity, signing up and meeting the people going takes three taps, and anyone can host an open event in seconds.

Process

1. Design, until June 2026

A high-fidelity interactive prototype with more than 20 screens connected end to end: onboarding, discover, event page, hosting, profiles, messaging, progress, payments and settings. Bilingual, with light and dark themes, and backed by a full design spec (colour, type, spacing, components, iconography, motion and navigation map) and a business plan.

Three principles: calm by default (content leads), a single accent (forest green carries every primary action) and human scale (large text, soft corners and touch targets of 44 pt or more).

2. From prototype to real app, since September 2026

The rule was to port the design, not redesign it. Work moves in short phases, each reviewed on a real iPhone before moving on: foundations, components, navigation, database, photos, discover, event page, accounts and joining.

Key decisions

  • Faithful to the design without copying by hand. Colours, all 738 strings and the icons are generated automatically from the prototype. If the design changes, they are regenerated.
  • The navigation map is data. The 77 connections between screens in the spec are encoded and checked automatically.
  • Privacy by design. Without an account you can see what's being played, never who. Data is hosted in the EU under the GDPR.
  • Conflict-proof spots. Free spots are calculated from who has joined, so two people can't take the last one at the same time.
  • No borrowed faces. The prototype avatars were Unsplash photos of real people; in the app they were replaced with initials.
  • Zero infrastructure cost as a project constraint.
  • Adjustments where mobile required them. The language follows the phone's, leaving an event has its own button with confirmation, and a broken contrast in dark mode was fixed.

Design system

16 colours across two themes, Manrope for the interface and Fraunces for the brand, line icons on a 24 × 24 grid, and required loading, empty, error and offline states on every screen.

Current status

Already working in the real app: discovering events in a list and on a map, search and filters (sport, when, level, price and distance), the event page, signing up and logging in, joining free events with a waiting list, and saving events.

Next steps: payments, event chat, hosting events, Google sign-in and then a pilot in Barcelona.
